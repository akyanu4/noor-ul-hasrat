A'udhu billahi min ash-shaytan ir-rajeem. Bismillah ir-Rahman ir-Raheem. Astaghfirullah, astaghfirullah al-'Azeem —
I seek the forgiveness of Allah, the Mighty, besides whom there is no god, the Living, the Self-Sustaining.

This is the research and treatise of Shams-ul-Mufassireen, Khadim-ul-Quran, Bahr-ul-Uloom, Hujjat-ul-Allama Hazrat
Muhammad Abdul Qadeer Siddiqui Hasrat, Rahmatullah Alaih, former Head of the Department of Theology at Osmania
University. It is Hazrat's treatise on **the reality of bai'at**, drawn from the Qur'an and the hadith. It is a very
important work of Hazrat's, written in his early years. On its first page is written:

> **Allah Ta'ala says:** *إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يُبَايِعُونَكَ إِنَّمَا يُبَايِعُونَ اللَّهَ يَدُ اللَّهِ فَوْقَ أَيْدِيهِمْ ۚ فَمَن نَّكَثَ فَإِنَّمَا يَنكُثُ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ ۖ وَمَنْ أَوْفَىٰ بِمَا عَاهَدَ عَلَيْهُ اللَّهَ فَسَيُؤْتِيهِ أَجْرًا عَظِيمًا*

That is: those who are giving bai'at at your hand, the Hand of Allah is over their hands. Those who are becoming
murids — the Hand of Allah is over their hands. See, this is a very great thing. And yet people do not want to give
bai'at. This is a verse of the Qur'an — Allah Ta'ala's own words. A time of great ruin has come.

> "**Bai'at and Iradat.** In the Holy Qur'an: *Indeed, those who pledge allegiance to you are pledging allegiance to
> Allah; the Hand of Allah is over their hands. So whoever breaks it breaks it only against himself, and whoever
> fulfils what he has promised Allah, He will soon give him a great reward* (Surah al-Fath, 48:10). That is: truly,
> those who take the pledge with you take it with Allah; Allah's Hand is over their hands. Then whoever breaks the
> pledge afterwards, its burden falls on himself; and whoever stays faithful to the promise made to Allah, Allah will
> soon grant him a great reward."

How great a thing this is — just look. Allahu Akbar! And yet people have abandoned it.

> "**What is bai'at, and what are its aims and purposes?**"

[reads it again] Now he gives the answer:

> "Bai'at is a covenant. It was a custom of the Arabs that when an agreement was made, they would strike hand upon
> hand. In every kingdom an oath of loyalty is taken; in the same way, bai'at is taken to confirm something important.
> In the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, when someone became Muslim he gave bai'at; when someone repented, bai'at
> was given. The bai'at of taqwa and tawbah was taken from women as well."

The bai'at of taqwa and tawbah — in the bai'at there is both: God-consciousness and repentance.

> "Sometimes bai'at was taken on shedding the last drop of one's blood, as happened at Hudaybiyyah."

Among the different battles and expeditions, this happened at Hudaybiyyah too. The Prophet ﷺ himself was present
there; that is why it is called a *ghazwa*. [reads it again] That is: "We will fight and fight until we die; fighting,
we will give even our lives." Where is that today? Nobody has it. Then what is the state of people's faith? And with
the plots of the Christians and Jews and others, people take their money and destroy everything.

> "Sometimes the Messenger of Allah ﷺ took bai'at on not asking anyone for anything. Such was the state of those
> people that if the rein of a camel fell, they would make the camel kneel and pick up the rein themselves, but they
> would not say to anyone else, 'Please hand me the rein.'"

When you are sitting on a camel, the rein is how you steer it — to turn, to run fast, to stop; it is all done with the
rein. If that rein fell, making the camel kneel and then picking it up is a great deal of trouble. Yet that is what
those people did — the Arabs, the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ.

> "Sometimes the Messenger of Allah ﷺ also took bai'at for remaining steadfast in the love of Allah and His Messenger."

Steadfast in the love of the Prophet ﷺ — see, you must love the Prophet ﷺ and also honour him: there is reverence,
and there is love.

> "In short, taking a pledge on something important is what is called bai'at."

> "**The Qur'an, the hadith and the books of fiqh are all in front of us — so what need is there to give bai'at at
> anyone's hand?**"

People ask this, and Hazrat answers it. When people said this to Hazrat in conversation, he first put their question
down here. [reads it again] Now he answers:

> "Think carefully and answer: two and two make four; if you take two from four, two remain. Arithmetic is such
> certain knowledge that hardly any other knowledge can be so certain and definite. Yet from the first class to the
> tenth, arithmetic is learned from teachers, and only then does one become skilled in it."

Even with knowledge this certain, you must study it from the first class to the tenth.

> "The real point is that a person cannot see his own mistakes. The teacher keeps catching the student's mistakes.
> When even in such obvious things a teacher is needed because of mistakes, then in subtle and delicate matters
> mistakes are far more frequent."

Far more frequent — that is, very many such mistakes can happen.

> "A teacher always keeps checking his students and warning them of their mistakes. When a teacher is needed in
> worldly work, then for *suluk* — for travelling the path of Allah — why would a spiritual teacher, a shaykh, not be
> needed?"

[reads it again] The spiritual path is a wondrous and strange matter. In it, it is essential that someone who knows
shows the way — a teacher who knows that knowledge thoroughly.

> "**Then why stay bound to the teaching of only one shaykh?**"

Whether someone is Qadri or Chishti — a Qadri takes from the Qadri way, a Chishti from the Chishti way. Here is the
answer:

> "Hearing the different ideas of different people leaves a person bewildered. For that reason one should make a
> trusted personality one's guide."

The one we rely on, we trust that from him we will receive correct knowledge, so we follow him and remain at peace
with that. Our *peshwa* — the one who shows us the way, who gives us guidance.

> "Think: if in an important court case a new lawyer were engaged every day, the case could not be pursued properly,
> nor could success be hoped for."

The lawyer prepares according to the whole of the law in order to answer.

> "Law books exist in Urdu — can you run a case by reading them yourself? No. In every work there is an expert; one
> must trust him and hand over all one's cases and affairs to him."

That is: you tell him everything — it is like this, and this, and this. Then he prepares it legally. You only tell
him what your circumstances are. [reads the passage again]

> "**How do the mashaikh usually give bai'at?** They take the men by the hand and take a pledge from them that they
> will live according to the command of Allah and His Messenger, and that as far as they can they will keep away from
> vices and hold fast to virtues."

We will keep away from bad things, from evils, and whatever is good, we will keep trying to act on it. [reads it again]

> "And if a person's teaching was not completed, the *iradat* of another shaykh is taken."

If one murshid could not give the complete teaching, people go to another murshid and receive it from him too. That
usually happens if our murshid could not give it, or has passed away — then one goes to someone else.

> "This is called being a *talib* (seeker)."

He was the murid of the first; now that he takes from this one, it is called being a talib.

> "Some people make friends with a good, experienced religious person, listen to his good advice and act on it. Such
> a person is called a *rafeeq-e-rah-e-Khuda*, a companion on the path of Allah. Such a person is not a murshid, but
> he is certainly an adviser."

> "**At the time of bai'at**, after saying the words of istighfar and of the pledge…"

The istighfar at bai'at — *Astaghfirullah al-'Azeem alladhi la ilaha illa Huwa al-Hayy al-Qayyum wa atubu ilayh* —
that is what we recite. And the words of the pledge: "I promise that, as far as I am able, I will walk according to
the commands of Allah and of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ." What is happening? A pledge is being made.

> "…they recite *Inna alladhina yubayi'unaka innama yubayi'un Allah…* to the end."

They recite the whole verse to the end.

> "And some shaykhs say: 'I counsel you with this verse:' *إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُ بِالْعَدْلِ وَالْإِحْسَانِ وَإِيتَاءِ ذِي
> الْقُرْبَىٰ وَيَنْهَىٰ عَنِ الْفَحْشَاءِ وَالْمُنكَرِ وَالْبَغْيِ ۚ يَعِظُكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ*"

> "After this the shaykh always keeps the murid under his care, and at all times, through his gracious attention, his
> spiritual help and the power of his thought and will, he keeps the murid away from bad things and keeps his good
> deeds and his intentions sound."

[reads the passage on the companion of the path again] *Rah-e-Khuda* is the path of Allah; such people are companions
on it — they keep you company and explain to you: this is so, and this is so. Such a person is not a murshid, but he
is certainly an adviser.

**What bai'at really is:** *"In bai'at, the chain of the tavern is joined to the chain of the 'Arsh."* When we were
reading hadith, in the lesson on hadith we were told — we heard the hadith — that from the 'Arsh, from Allah, a rope
hangs down to the earth, and it is in the blessed hand of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. From it the Prophet ﷺ guides the
Companions and others, and it stays in their hands. It is a very great thing: the moment one becomes a murid, a
connection with the 'Arsh comes into being.

[reads again the istighfar, the verse of bai'at and the verse of counsel] — act on it, yes, it is to be acted on.

[reads again the shaykh's care of the murid] So with the knowledge of the Qur'an and hadith, and with his own will
too — the shaykh does both. "At all times through his gracious attention, his spiritual help and the power of his
thought" — see how many things are used! — "by his will he keeps the murid from bad things and keeps his good deeds
and intentions sound."

> "**Sometimes bai'at is taken even from small children** who do not yet understand. This is called *bai'at-e-barakat*,
> the bai'at of blessing."

When our Azeem Baba — my son — was born, and was a little bigger, Hazrat Madeen Pasha — my father-in-law, who is also
my maternal uncle; my wife was there too — took the child into the house and said: "Bhai jaan, please make him your
murid. Bhai jaan, please make him your murid." I said, "He is very small." He said, "What does that matter? Bhai
jaan, please take him." "Very well," I said, and I made him a murid; I took him, and straight away passed my hand over
him.

> "Sometimes bai'at is taken from small children who do not understand; this is called bai'at-e-barakat. That was
> about the bai'at of men. **In the bai'at of women**, giving bai'at by word of mouth alone is enough."

Now the woman sits in front, the murshid sits in front: "Look, I take this bai'at from you; be steadfast." "I am
steadfast."

> "The elders of the faith hold one corner of their handkerchief or shawl themselves and give the other corner to the
> woman to hold."

We do the same; the Qadri elders did the same, and I do the same.

> "Some keep water in a basin; on one side is the shaykh's hand and on the other side the hand of the woman becoming a
> murid. Most women are unveiled before an aged, elderly shaykh, but it is not at all necessary for the woman becoming
> a murid to be unveiled before the shaykh, however old and elderly he may be."

[reads it again] They remain properly covered, in their veil; I say nothing about it.

> "**What should the murid's age be at the time of bai'at?**"

What is bai'at? Becoming a murid — that is bai'at.

> "Some say he should be an adult. And some say he should have understanding and insight — able to tell good from bad."

*Understanding* (fahm): when something is said, it makes sense to him. *Insight* (firasat): taking the knowledge he has
been given, at once finding the way to act on it, getting to work, and doing it well.

The meaning of *Inna Allaha ya'muru…*: "Truly, Allah commands you to justice and to excellence, and to giving to
relatives, and forbids you from indecency, from what is wrong, and from transgression. He counsels you so that you
may take heed." See what kind of teaching this is. Becoming a murid is proven from the Qur'an.

**Those who do not become murids** draw close to Shaytan. Shaytan can lead them astray whenever he wishes, because no
wali is with them. The shade of a wali is not over them; they are not holding the hand of a wali; that rope is not in
their hand.

> "**What should the shaykh be like?** He should have the ability to fulfil the aim and purpose of discipleship. Some
> shaykhs hold that the murshid should know some of the verses of the Qur'an's commands, and the necessary matters of
> hadith and fiqh, and should have been blessed by the company of some experienced personality."

That is: being a khalifa. What should a shaykh be? The word *shaykh* itself means murshid. A shaykh is not someone who
has learned neither tajweed nor the Qur'an. There are so many subtleties in reciting by the rules of tajweed that a
qari is prepared only after many years — a true qari. And the meanings change with a tiny mark: *inna* — the *nun*
has a *zabar* and a *shadda* — and if the zabar is lengthened, it becomes something else; the meaning goes from one
place to another.

Permission to give *khilafat* comes from above — from the murshids, from Ghaus-e-Pak; to Ghaus-e-Pak it comes from
Hazrat 'Ali (may Allah be pleased with him), and to him from the Prophet ﷺ.

> [reads again what the shaykh should be like] "Some elders have given this mark of a good murshid: **in whose company
> all whisperings and useless thoughts stop, and a person's mind becomes bound to God and His Messenger.**"

Now look — you can see it for yourselves; you can understand it yourselves. In whose company the whisperings and
idle thoughts all stop, and one's thoughts become attached to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ — see?
