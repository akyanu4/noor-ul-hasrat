## Recording 1 (001-HB, 52:54)

**00:00** A'udhu billahi min ash-shaytan ir-rajeem. Bismillah ir-Rahman ir-Raheem. Astaghfirullah al-'Azeem alladhi la ilaha illa Huwa al-Hayy al-Qayyum wa atubu ilayh.  
**00:17** Bismillah. This is the research and treatise of Shams-ul-Mufassireen, Khadim-ul-Quran, Bahr-ul-Uloom, Hujjat-ul-Allama Hazrat Muhammad Abdul Qadeer Siddiqui Hasrat Rahmatullah Alaih, Professor and former Head of the Department of Theology, Osmania University — Hazrat's treatise on the reality of bai'at, from the Qur'an and the hadith. It is a very important work, written in his early years.  
**00:55** On its first page is written: Allah Ta'ala says —  
**01:06** 'Innal-ladhina yubayi'unaka innama yubayi'un Allah; yadullahi fawqa aydihim…' (al-Fath 48:10)  
**01:25** That is: those who give bai'at at your hand — the Hand of Allah is over their hands. Those who become murids — the Hand of Allah is over their hand. See, this is a very great thing — and yet people do not want to give bai'at.  
**01:52** It is a verse of the Qur'an. A time of great ruin has come. [He reads the title page: Haqeeqat-e-Bai'at, third printing, by Shams-ul-Mufassireen, Khadim-ul-Quran.]  
**02:11** Bismillah. 'Bai'at and Iradat. In the Holy Qur'an: "Indeed, those who pledge allegiance to you pledge allegiance to Allah; the Hand of Allah is over their hands. Whoever breaks it breaks it against himself, and whoever fulfils what he has pledged to Allah, He will give him a great reward." — Surah al-Fath,  
**02:39** verse 10. That is: truly, those who take bai'at with you take it with Allah; Allah's Hand is over their hands. Whoever breaks the pledge after bai'at, its burden falls on himself; and whoever stays faithful to his promise to Allah, Allah will soon give him a great reward.' How great a thing this is — just look, Allahu Akbar!  
**03:17** But people have abandoned it. 'So what is bai'at, and what are its aims and purposes?'  
**03:26** Now the answer: 'Bai'at is a covenant. It was a custom of the Arabs that when an agreement was made, they struck hand upon hand. In every kingdom an oath of loyalty is taken; in the same way, bai'at is taken to confirm something important.'  
**03:54** 'In the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, when someone became Muslim he gave bai'at; when someone repented, bai'at was taken. The bai'at of taqwa and tawbah was taken from women too.' In bai'at there is both: taqwa and tawbah.  
**04:23** 'Sometimes bai'at was taken on shedding the last drop of one's blood, as happened at Hudaybiyyah.' Among the battles — the ghazwat — it happened at Hudaybiyyah; the Prophet ﷺ himself was there, which is why it is called a ghazwa.  
**04:44** 'Sometimes bai'at was taken on shedding the last drop of one's blood' — that is: 'We will fight and fight until we die; fighting, we will give even our lives.'  
**05:02** Where is that today? Nobody has it. Then what is the state of people's faith? Through the plots of the Christians, Jews and others, people take money and do who knows what, and have ruined everything.  
**05:23** 'Sometimes bai'at was taken on shedding the last drop of blood, as at Hudaybiyyah. And sometimes the Messenger of Allah ﷺ took bai'at on not asking anyone for anything.'  
**05:36** 'Such was their state that if a camel's rein fell, they would make the camel kneel and pick it up themselves — but they would not ask anyone, "Please hand me the rein."'  
**05:52** When you sit on a camel, the rein is how you steer it — to turn, to run, to stop. If the rein fell, making the camel kneel and picking it up is a great deal of trouble — yet that is what they did, the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ.  
**06:18** 'Sometimes the Messenger of Allah ﷺ also took bai'at for remaining steadfast in the love of Allah and His Messenger.' Steadfast in the love of the Prophet ﷺ — you must love the Prophet ﷺ, and honour him too. 'In short, taking a pledge on something important is called bai'at.'  
**06:47** 'The Qur'an, the hadith and the books of fiqh are all in front of us — so why give bai'at at anyone's hand?' People ask this, and Hazrat answers it; when people said this to him, he first wrote their question down.  
**07:30** Now the answer: 'Think carefully: two and two make four; take two from four and two remain. Arithmetic is such certain knowledge that hardly any other is so certain. Yet from the first class to the tenth it is learned from teachers, and only then does one master it.'  
**08:16** 'The real point is that a person cannot see his own mistakes; the teacher keeps catching the student's mistakes. When even in such obvious things a teacher is needed, then in subtle and delicate matters mistakes are far more frequent. A teacher always watches over his students and keeps warning them of their mistakes.'  
**08:51** 'When a teacher is needed in worldly work, then for travelling the path of Allah, why would a spiritual teacher — a shaykh — not be needed?'  
**09:19** [reads it again] The spiritual path is a wondrous matter; in it, it is essential that someone who knows shows the way — a teacher who knows that knowledge thoroughly.  
**09:46** 'Then why stay bound to the teaching of one shaykh?' Whether Qadri or Chishti — a Qadri takes from the Qadri way, a Chishti from the Chishti way. Here is the answer:  
**10:11** 'Hearing the different views of different people leaves a person bewildered; so one should make a trusted personality one's guide.' The one we trust, we are sure we will get correct knowledge from him, so we rely on him and stay at peace — our guide, who shows us the way.  
**10:44** 'Think: if in an important court case a new lawyer were engaged every day, the case could not be pursued properly, nor could success be hoped for.' Lawyers prepare according to the whole of the law in order to answer.  
**11:17** 'Law books exist in Urdu — can you run a case by reading them? No. In every work there is an expert; trust him and hand over all your cases and affairs to him.'  
**11:50** That is: you tell him everything — it is like this, and this — and he prepares it legally; you only tell him your circumstances. [He reads the passage about the lawyer again.]  
**12:28** 'How do the mashaikh usually give bai'at? They take the men by the hand and take a pledge from them that they will live according to the command of Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, and as far as they can will keep away from vices and hold fast to virtues.'  
**12:56** We will keep away from bad things, and whatever is good we will try to act on. 'And if a person's teaching was not completed, the iradat of another shaykh is taken.'  
**13:19** If one murshid could not give the complete teaching, people go to another murshid too — that usually happens if our murshid could not give it, or has passed away; then one goes to someone else.  
**13:42** 'This is called being a talib — a seeker.' He was the murid of the first; now that he takes from this one, it is called being a talib.  
**14:04** 'Some people make friends with a good, experienced religious person, listen to his good advice and act on it. Such a person is called a companion on the path of Allah — not a murshid, but certainly an adviser.'  
**14:31** 'At the time of bai'at, after the words of istighfar and the pledge —' the istighfar we recite at bai'at, and the words of the pledge: 'I promise that, as far as I can, I will walk according to the commands of Allah and of His Messenger ﷺ.' What is happening? A pledge is being made.  
**15:12** '…they recite Innal-ladhina yubayi'unaka… to the end' — the whole verse. 'And some shaykhs say: I counsel you with this verse —'  
**15:28** 'Inna Allaha ya'muru bil-'adli wal-ihsan, wa ita'i dhil-qurba, wa yanha 'anil-fahsha'i wal-munkari wal-baghy; ya'izukum la'allakum tadhakkarun.' (an-Nahl 16:90)  
**15:49** 'After this the shaykh always keeps the murid in his care, and at all times, by his gracious attention, his spiritual help and the power of his thought and will, keeps him from bad things and keeps his deeds and intentions sound.'  
**16:09** [reads again] 'A companion on the path of Allah' — the path of Allah, and these are companions on it: they keep you company and explain things to you. Such a person is not a murshid, but certainly an adviser.  
**16:43** What bai'at really is: 'In bai'at, the chain of the tavern is joined to the chain of the 'Arsh.' When we were reading hadith we were taught that from the 'Arsh, from Allah, a rope hangs down to the earth, and it is in the blessed hand of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ; with it he guides the Companions and others, and it stays in their hands.  
**17:27** It is a very great thing: the moment one becomes a murid, a connection with the 'Arsh comes into being. [He reads again the verse of bai'at and the verse of counsel.]  
**18:07** 'After this the shaykh always keeps the murid in his care, and at all times, by his gracious attention, spiritual help and power of thought and will, keeps him from bad things and keeps his deeds and intentions sound.'  
**18:30** So with his knowledge of the Qur'an and hadith, and with his own will too — the shaykh uses both. 'At all times, by his gracious attention, his spiritual help and the power of his thought' — see how many things are used!  
**19:00** 'Sometimes bai'at is taken even from small children who do not yet understand; this is called bai'at-e-barakat — the bai'at of blessing.'  
**19:12** When our Azeem Baba — my son — had been born and was a little bigger, Hazrat Mohiuddin Pasha — my father-in-law, who is also my maternal uncle; my wife was there too — took the child in his lap and said: 'Baba jaan, please make him your murid.' 'He is very small.' 'What does that matter, Baba jaan? Please take him.'  
**19:54** 'Very well,' I said, and I took him, and at once passed my hand over him. 'Sometimes bai'at is taken from small children; this is called bai'at-e-barakat.'  
**20:08** 'That was the bai'at of men. In the bai'at of women, giving bai'at by word of mouth alone is enough.' The woman sits in front, the murshid sits in front: 'Look, I take this bai'at from you; be steadfast on it.' And she says, 'I am steadfast.'  
**20:35** 'The elders of the faith hold one corner of their handkerchief or shawl and give the other corner to the woman to hold.' We do the same; the elders before us did the same, and I do the same.  
**20:55** 'Some keep water in a basin, the shaykh's hand on one side and the woman's on the other. Most women are unveiled before an aged, elderly shaykh, but it is not at all necessary for the woman becoming a murid to be unveiled before the shaykh, however old he may be.'  
**21:20** 'What should the murid's age be at bai'at? Some say an adult; some say he should have understanding and insight.' Understanding: when something is said, it makes sense to him. Insight: taking the knowledge given, at once finding the way to act on it, getting to work, and doing it well.  
**21:51** [He reads again: the bai'at of women by word of mouth, the corner of the shawl, the basin of water, and that a woman need not be unveiled.]  
**22:25** Some, even after becoming murids, stay in their burqa; I say nothing to them about it.  
**22:34** 'What should the murid's age be at bai'at?' What is bai'at? Becoming a murid — that is bai'at. 'Some say he should be an adult, and some that he should have understanding and insight — able to tell good from bad.'  
**22:57** Inna Allaha ya'muru — its meaning: 'Truly, Allah commands you to justice and to excellence and to giving to relatives, and forbids indecency, what is wrong and transgression. He counsels you so that you may take heed.' See what kind of teaching this is.  
**23:31** Becoming a murid is proven from the Qur'an, yet people do not become murids — and then they draw close to Shaytan. Shaytan can lead them astray whenever he wishes, because no wali is with them: the shade of a wali is not over them, they are not holding a wali's hand, that rope is not in their hand.  
**24:00** 'So what should the shaykh be like? He should have the ability to fulfil the aim and purpose of discipleship. Some shaykhs hold that the murshid should know some of the verses of the Qur'an's commands and the necessary matters of hadith and fiqh, and should have been blessed by the company of an experienced personality.'  
**24:25** That is: being a khalifa. What should a shaykh be? The word shaykh itself means murshid. A shaykh is not one who has learned neither tajweed nor the Qur'an. Reciting by the rules of tajweed has so many subtleties that a true qari is prepared only after many years — and a tiny mark changes the meaning: on the nun of 'inna' there is a zabar and a shadda, and if the zabar is lengthened it becomes something else.  
**25:13** Say it wrongly and the meaning goes somewhere else entirely. Permission to give khilafat comes from above — from the murshids, from Ghaus-e-Pak; to Ghaus-e-Pak from Hazrat Ali (may Allah be pleased with him), and to him from the Prophet ﷺ.  
**25:36** 'What should the shaykh be like? [reads again] Some elders have given this mark of a good murshid: in whose company all whisperings and idle thoughts stop —'  
**26:10** Now look — you can see it yourselves; you can understand it yourselves. 'In whose company all whisperings and idle thoughts stop, and a person's mind becomes bound to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ.' See?  
**26:37** What a difference it has made! Nowadays there is no such teaching. The teaching we had here has come to an end — all that is left is sitting in qawwali.  
**26:50** Many people do zikr. In zikr one draws closer to Allah; one remembers Allah, and the thought stays turned towards Allah.  
**27:02** But it stays far off. Only qawwali — they listen to qawwali the whole night.  
**27:08** In it is the mention of the Prophet ﷺ, his glory and greatness; the love for the Prophet ﷺ is intense.  
**27:17** That is necessary in its place. But if that is all there is, how far does one get?  
**27:24** Some elders have given this mark of a good murshid: in whose company all whisperings and idle thoughts stop, and a person's mind becomes bound to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ.  
**27:38** Look first and understand, then give your hand. Otherwise regret follows.  
**27:46** The one who frees you from bondage to everything else — his company is necessary.  
**27:54** If he turns your thoughts towards Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, that is the real thing.  
**28:02** To see good and bad for what they are is the eye of a great one.  
**28:07** Now see, what do I always tell you all? When I speak, keep looking into my eyes. Some people sit with their heads down while I talk, so I tell them at once: look into my eyes.  
**28:32** The states that come out of my eyes come into your eyes too. That is why you should keep your eyes open and keep looking at the murshid.  
**28:48** The Companions used to keep looking at the Prophet ﷺ — and they also lowered their gaze out of respect.  
**28:53** To see good and bad for what they are is the eye of a great one.  
**29:00** When the heart is straight, the eyes stay fixed on one point; the eye of a troubled person stays troubled too.  
**29:11** Some say that if you look at a wali from behind, he often turns round and looks; and if you ask him something, he answers at once.  
**29:22** There is an incident. The Chadarghat bridge, towards Malakpet — after crossing the bridge, on the way to Sultan Bazar, there is a mosque on the left-hand side.  
**29:40** In that mosque people were sitting for namaz and talking about something. An old man, a wali, was sitting there, and everyone was listening to him.  
**30:04** It so happened that Hazrat Qadeer Piya Sarkar was entering the mosque by the door. As he stepped onto the stairs, the old wali threw his staff and said: 'If anyone has the strength, let him pick it up!'  
**30:36** Qadeer Piya Sarkar caught it. 'Oh — it is you who has come!' He had picked it up.  
**30:42** Why? Without turning round, the old wali knew that a wali was behind him — that is why he threw his staff. 'Oh, it is you! How else would I know you had come?'  
**31:10** Did you see? It says here: a wali recognises a wali. If a wali is behind him, he turns and looks — he knows someone is there.  
**31:40** Remember: the thoughts of every person and the effect of his company draw others in and spread all around — like a stone thrown into water: the ripples come.  
**32:27** Good company leads to good, and bad company leads to evil. Throw one pebble into water and it makes ripples; a circle forms.  
**32:50** Good company leads to good, bad company to evil. But if a big stone and a small pebble are thrown into the water together, the big stone's ripples swallow the pebble's ripples.  
**33:19** Do you understand? The small pebble's ripples are gone; the big stone's are at work — they overpower them.  
**33:31** In the same way, a person of ordinary thoughts and weak faith who sits in the company of a perfected one — his doubtful and harmful thoughts grow weaker and weaker.  
**33:53** He gains the strength to leave his weak thoughts; all the bad thoughts fade away, and he is shaped by the company of the perfected, strong-willed one.  
**38:46** If someone makes a mistake after giving bai'at, he should repent and say: Astaghfirullah al-'Azeem alladhi la ilaha illa Huwa al-Hayy al-Qayyum wa atubu ilayh. Recite this much.  
**38:58** Even if a sinless person repents, it is good for him. Repentance never goes to waste.  
**39:14** Once a Companion had given bai'at at the hand of the Prophet ﷺ. After a while the Prophet ﷺ said: 'You too, give bai'at.' He said: 'O Messenger of Allah ﷺ, I have already given bai'at.' The Prophet ﷺ said: 'What of it? Give bai'at again.'  
**39:36** SubhanAllah. 'What of it? Give bai'at again.' See how it is.  
**39:42** Where else would you find this? It looks like a small book, but it is full of knowledge you won't find in big books — here it is, in a few pages.  
**39:56** [He reads the passage again: repentance after bai'at, and the Companion told to give bai'at again.]  
**40:25** Let me give you another example. I have studied hadith; I have read a great deal of hadith.  
**40:39** Once it was the time of the Isha prayer. The Prophet ﷺ was sitting with his Companions. As the prayer began everyone stood up — but one of them stayed sitting.  
**41:01** He said: 'I have already given bai'at.' 'What of it? Give bai'at again — otherwise it is of no use.'  
**41:26** 'Do not stay apart; give bai'at.' Do you understand? This is how it is: once you have given bai'at, renew it. That is what I do — having given bai'at once, I give it again.  
**41:47** Is it necessary for every person to become a murid? The answer: whoever considers it necessary to live by the rules of the path and wants to fulfil them at a high level. If you do not consider it necessary, then deprivation is your lot.  
**42:08** There is an old saying: 'Whoever has no pir, his pir is Shaytan.' Do you see? This is very important.  
**42:26** And it is easy to see why: the murid has his master's hand over him, so Shaytan cannot touch him.  
**43:58** Now I see something — that is a different matter. Qadeer Piya Sarkar fixed his eyes on mine and looked at me, and kept telling me many things. I held his gaze; I kept looking, I kept listening.  
**44:21** For years — from childhood until I married and had children. I was in Warangal; I have two children, Azeem Baba and Khwaja Baba. In Warangal I used to go about with Hazrat.  
**44:46** Does it go to waste? People were Muslims, but when they saw the Prophet ﷺ, what did they become? Sahabis — Companions.  
**45:03** Now no wali can reach their rank, however great — because they looked at the Prophet ﷺ with their own eyes, and the Prophet ﷺ looked at them. Do you understand what being a Companion is?  
**45:28** So what is happening? Through the eyes, the voice, the warmth, the breath — through so many things — sitting with a wali, what spreads from his body, his heart, his mind and his thoughts reaches you. It does not reach others; those who are with him are the ones affected.  
**46:01** Is it necessary for everyone? Whoever hates bad things and keeps away from them, who is drawn to good things, wants to see and hear good and become good himself, and considers it necessary to keep the rules of the path at a high level — [for him it is]. If you do not consider it necessary, you are deprived.  
**46:49** To those who raise objections, Hazrat gives this answer too: this is the saying of the elders.  
**47:06** Someone may also say: the books of the Qur'an and hadith are available, the books of tasawwuf and Sunnah are available — then why did different ways arise: Qadri, Chishti, Naqshbandi and so on?  
**47:26** The one who derives rulings from the Qur'an and hadith is a faqih, a jurist: from the verses of the Qur'an and the practice of the Prophet ﷺ he says, 'This is a matter of fiqh; act on it.' What is fiqh? Looking at the verses of the Qur'an and the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ: this is the ruling.  
**48:17** The answer is: just as the jurists who derive rulings from the Qur'an and hadith differ — some Hanafi, some Shafi'i, some Maliki, some Hanbali — so too those who derive the path from the Qur'an and hadith are Qadri, Chishti, Naqshbandi and so on. MashaAllah, SubhanAllah.  
**48:44** Now look: when we do wudu from the Qur'an, what do we do? After the washing we do masah — we wipe the head. In that we are Hanafi: we wipe a quarter of the head.  
**49:15** Another school wipes the whole head — is it the Hanbali or the Shafi'i? Perhaps the Hanbali wipe the whole head, and the Shafi'i just a little. We wipe a quarter.  
**49:43** This is fiqh. In the Qur'an, what is it? 'Wipe your heads' — whatever the words for it are — and one school does it this way, another that way.  
**50:00** Those who studied the hadith and practised them understood the questions of fiqh and began to act on them; those who follow a particular way are different: Hanafi, Shafi'i, Hanbali — all of them are like this.  
**50:31** Some, even in prayer, do it like this — raising the hands; it is called raf'-ul-yadain. The Hanafis do not do raf'-ul-yadain; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis do. See how far we have come.  
**50:50** In the same way there are those who chose a path: Qadri, Chishti, Naqshbandi and so on. So what are we all? People who have chosen a path and are walking on it.  
**51:12** Let me read another line. Remember one thing: people's temperaments differ; their natures are different. Some are drawn to one way and some to another; each finds his own way, and for each there is a way. Different families of the path have different ways.  
**52:36** See, it is Allah's great grace: He gives to whom He wills. And so much knowledge [in so few pages].

**00:33** And may you know that the dars here can also be seen. What is it? Kashf — it opens. Here, by reading the teachings of the Prophet ﷺ, kashf comes too. Do you understand? And from above, ilqa — inspiration — also opens. This is Allah's grace.  
**01:09** A'udhu billah, Bismillah. In Haqeeqat-e-Bai'at, Huzoor Shams-ul-Mufassireen, Khadim-ul-Quran, Bahr-ul-Uloom Hazrat Muhammad Abdul Qadeer Siddiqui Hasrat Rahmatullah Alaih, Professor and former Head of the Department of Theology, Osmania University — published by the Hasrat Academy long ago; this is its third printing.  
**01:50** Hazrat writes: 'Every Muslim man and woman should do istighfar — so why is a murshid needed for istighfar?'  
**02:17** Such objections and questions are answered in the Qur'an. Now see, this is written on a verse of the Qur'an: 'If, when they had wronged themselves, they had come to you and asked Allah's forgiveness, and the Messenger had asked forgiveness for them, they would have found Allah Accepting of repentance, Most Merciful.' (an-Nisa 4:64)  
**03:11** It is clear from this that asking forgiveness on one's own is not enough; the prayer of one whose heart is enlightened should also be sought — just as the Prophet ﷺ himself would pray for their forgiveness.  
**03:58** 'May Allah forgive their sins.' Becoming a murid begins with istighfar: the murshid prays for forgiveness for him, and there is hope that it will be accepted.  
**04:22** Just think: love for the sake of Allah. It is a very important thing; it is in the hadith: for those who love one another for Allah's sake, pulpits of light will be set up — a station so high that the prophets will say, 'Oh, what a station!'  
**05:16** Think with a calm heart: why does the murid love his murshid? He loves him for the sake of Allah. Love for Allah's sake is what is between the two. Oh! With Allah, the matter of love goes so far!  
**06:18** Don't you know? 'A person is with the one he loves.' And you are with the one you love.  
**06:50** There is an address in this: someone came and asked, and he was told — the Companion was told — 'A person will be with the one he loves.'  
**07:09** And it is in the Holy Qur'an: even if the children fall short in deeds, Allah joins them with their parents in Paradise. (at-Tur 52:21)  
**07:37** Who are these murids? They are spiritual children. Wow! InshaAllah, the murids will be joined with their master.  
**07:56** Wow! Khwaja ya Sarkar, Khwaja ya Sarkar, Ghaus-e-Pak Sarkar! SubhanAllah, MashaAllah.  
**08:12** What is being read now is not just for sitting and listening — you have to act on it at once. That is why you are rushing forward, and I joined you at once.  
**08:27** So what am I doing? I am meeting you, but I am giving my hand to Qadeer Piya. I see myself there. Why are you looking at me? I am looking there, not at you — at my master.  
**08:44** MashaAllah — wow, wow! Everyone has joined hands. MashaAllah. May you live long!  
**09:00** Now what are you doing? You have started meeting me — and through me, Qadeer Piya. He is my master, so you are happy that you met our master. He is the master of the master — the Dada, Dada Peer. That is why we give it attention.  
**09:28** Who are these murids? Spiritual children. InshaAllah, the murids will be joined with their master; they will rise with him.  
**09:43** They will rise with him from the grave. As soon as they rise they will say: 'Where is my master?' and they will go to their master.  
**10:07** I don't know how many thousands, how many lakhs of murids will follow their master — think of it — they will be in crores. Qadeer Piya had lakhs of murids; now his murids have lakhs of murids. MashaAllah. Where the master will be, the murids will be too, InshaAllah.  
**10:34** What is khilafat? Now listen. Everyone thinks it is a fine thing to have khilafat, but it is a great thing — it comes from above. One has to think: am I worthy of it? Do I have that station, that connection with Allah and His Messenger ﷺ? He may think it, but he should not ask; he should stay silent. Allah gives what He wills.  
**11:29** When we ask once, Allah does not give; even twice, He does not. The third time it comes sternly: 'I am telling you — you are to give him khilafat, and you are not giving it?' 'Give it to him.' 'Yes, sir.' And he gave it. MashaAllah.  
**11:57** So what is khilafat? It is the confirmation that a murid has become worthy.

**15:46** The one who follows the one before him and carries on his work is his khalifa — his successor.  
**16:02** From the Messenger of Allah ﷺ the Muslims received the Qur'an and the hadith, and through the blessing of his company their selves were purified — our nafs became pure.  
**16:17** Tazkiya-e-nafs means the purifying of our self.  
**16:22** Whoever recites the Holy Qur'an to people and teaches others to read it correctly — that is, the qari — he too is a khalifa.  
**16:43** Whoever explains the meanings of the Holy Qur'an — that is, writes tafsir — is a mufassir: a khalifa.  
**16:53** Whoever narrates the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ and, after verifying them, passes them on to the Ummah — that is, the muhaddith — he too is a khalifa.  
**17:03** Whoever derives rulings from the Qur'an and hadith — draws them out in the light of the Qur'an and hadith: 'this is the ruling' —  
**17:23** that is, the mujtahid and the faqih — he too is a khalifa.  
**17:37** A faqih, you see, takes the verses of the Qur'an and the hadith related to them and says: we should act according to fiqh. That is fiqh, and the one who does it is the faqih — the mujtahid and faqih; he too is a khalifa.

**18:53** He is a king, but he too becomes a khalifa: he walks on Islam and makes the people keep to it.  
**19:00** Whoever explains the secrets of the deen — the deen has secrets, hidden things that cannot be grasped outwardly, and he explains those too — whoever purifies the self, corrects the thoughts and teaches spirituality: he too is a khalifa.

**20:28** Whoever teaches soldiering is also a khalifa. We had training in all of that too — binnot (stick-fighting), wrestling and so on.  
**20:41** In it there was the strike Hazrat Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) used to make with his sword — the zarb-e-Haidari, as it is called. People could not get it.  
**20:57** A man became Hazrat's murid and came. After a while he said that he did binnot, sword and all these things. 'Is that so? Do you know the zarb-e-Haidari?' 'Yes, I know it.'  
**21:14** 'Then show me.' At once he drew and struck, exactly as it should be done — he showed the zarb-e-Haidari. Oh!  
**21:27** Then Hazrat said: 'In this, you have become my teacher. In this, you have become my teacher.' That is how it was — the zarb-e-Haidari.  
**21:41** And once it happened that he was showing us how to wrestle and what to do.  
**21:48** And besides that he taught us: when you fight with the stick, with a sword or with a staff, don't look here and there — no — keep looking into the eyes of the one you are fighting. Don't look here and there.  
**22:09** So when we fought, that is what we did: we kept looking at him.  
**22:17** In the same way I tell you: when I say something, open your eyes and look at me — don't sit with your head bowed out of respect, not at all. From my gaze into your gaze, into your eyes, a state will pass from here.
